 AS-IS SITUATION  
After sanding down the old antifouling I noticed that the rudder had some cracks at the bottom 
mounting.  
I discussed that with the AMEL forum, with an AMEL surveyor and also AMEL directly.  
The answers were different: from “it is not necessary to repair”, “we sailed with those cracks 
30,000NM and nothing happened” to “it has to be repaired”. I decided to repair these cracks because 
the rudder is one of the most important things aboard for safety.

bearing are bolted through. Be careful when you reach the screws not to sand them down.

Figure 7 - The quadrant in the aft cabine which has to release from the top shaft  
 RUDDER REMOVAL  
4.1  OUTSIDE  
To remove and handle the rudder easily you need as minimum 2 strong people, better would be 
three. All positioned outside. Inside no one is required but you can positioning an additionall person 
inside.  
Be sure that you have enough space between the rudder and the ground to allow the rudder slipping 
down. You need as minimum ca. 60cm clearance between the rudder and the ground.  
Support the rudder with wooden blocks and a vehicle jack.  
Remove all bolts of the middle and the bottom bearing.  
You need to open up the U-shaped middle bearing stainless steel stripe (see “Figure 10 - The 
opened up U-shaped middle bearing stainless steel stripe”). For that you have to unscrew the small 
bolts.

 THE REPAIR  
Do all necessary repairs using proper methods: clean down to sane material, rebuild using resin fiber 
cloth before protecting.  
My rudder was repaired by a professional company.  
When sanding down to sane material the repair shop found out that the rudder had some bubbles in 
the GRP. They must be from the initial building.  
They used two layers of Biax fiberglass. One big layer for both sides. The layers were not cut in the 
front of the rudder: each fiberglass layer was placed around in the front of the rudder.

 PAINTING  
Before replacing back the rudder you should take the chance to paint as minimum all areas where 
you cannot reach when the rudder is reinstalled, i.e. the skeg where the front part of the rudder will 
cover it, the front side of rudder itself.   
I painted the complete skeg (w/o the area which you have to laminate back) with 3 layers of epoxy 
primer and than one layer of antifouling.  
I also organized a spray gun to spray epoxy primer into the rudder to protect the repaired areas 
inside. For that I used the holes in the top and bottom and the both holes for the zink anodes (port 
and starboard side).

7.2  INSIDE  
Also inside your have to re-install the sealant packing, the nylon nut and the quadrant.   
 
For the shaft packing  I used 70cm of PTFE Flax shaft packing 5/16” which have to cut into three 
pieces. Cut the corners at an angle for a better sealing. 
I never remember the length of each part but you can use the old packings or the hole for 
measurement.  
When installing the new packing coat them with silicon grease.  
 
Figure 33 - The packing has to cut at an angle

When taking into place be sure that the cuts are as fare away from each other for better sealing: 
 
Figure 34 – The three shaft packings has to insert into place like above  
 
 
Screwing the nylon nut after each navigation until well tightened (but not too much!).  
Check bolts on square section regularly, they could come unfastened with rudder motion if not 
checked. 
 
 FINAL WORK  
The final work is to bring the open areas at the bearings and between in shape:  
1.  First I used epoxy primer to fill the area of the bearings and the wire. Sand it down to nearly 
the final shape.  
2.  Then I overlayed the areas with a fiberglass mat and sanded as-well.  
3.  Use the epoxy primer for a last final adjustment of the final shape.  
4.  Paint it with epoxy primer and than with antifouling.
