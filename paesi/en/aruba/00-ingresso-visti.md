# 00 — Entry, Documents & Visas

**Last checked: 27/08/2026**

## Status

Aruba is an **autonomous constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands** (status *land*), with its own government and its own immigration and visa rules, separate from the European Netherlands and from the other Dutch Caribbean countries (Curaçao, Bonaire). It is **outside the EU and outside the Schengen Area**: EU/Schengen rules do not apply to entry into Aruba. [netherlandsworldwide.nl — Caribbean visa ★★★★★; visitaruba.com ★★★★★]

## EU Citizens — No Visa Required

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Visa | **Not required** for EU citizens (including Italy) among the visa-exempt countries for the Caribbean parts of the Kingdom; the official exempt list includes Italy, France, Germany etc. | netherlandsworldwide.nl “Do I need a visa for the Caribbean parts of the Kingdom?” — list of exempt countries ★★★★★ |
| Required documents | **Passport** not older than 10 years, **valid for the entire stay**; **return or onward ticket**; **approved ED Card**; proof of accommodation or ownership (including a pleasure yacht ≥14 m at the waterline); proof of sufficient means and medical insurance if required; documents for re-entry (residence permit/visa if needed for the country of return) | netherlandsworldwide.nl “Requirements for travelling without a visa” ★★★★★; visitaruba.com “Required entry documents” ★★★★★; edcardaruba.aw ★★★★★ |
| Standard stay | **30 days** on first admission as a tourist (the officer notes the days on the ED card); **maximum total 180 days per calendar year** as a tourist | visitaruba.com “Duration of admission” ★★★★★; aruba.com immigration regulations ★★★★★ |
| Linked visa exemptions | Those holding a **valid residence permit** for the USA/Canada/Schengen/UK/Ireland or a **multiple-entry Schengen/USA/UK visa** (for certain nationalities: Bolivia, China, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Haiti, India, Jamaica, Peru etc.) are exempt from the Caribbean visa even if their nationality would otherwise require it | netherlandsworldwide.nl ★★★★★; visitaruba.com “Visa required persons exempted” ★★★★★ |

⚠️ **Caution**: final admission is always at the discretion of the border officer at the port of entry; even with an approved ED card and no visa, entry may be refused or limited to fewer days if requirements are not met or in case of a *blacklist*. Holding a visa does not guarantee entry. [visitaruba.com ★★★★★]

## Important Note for Arrival by Sea

- **People** (IASA — Immigration) and **yacht** (Customs/Douane) controls are **separate but both mandatory** at the same port of entry: Immigration first, then Customs. The people formality requires the **ED Card online for every crew member** (not a visa, to be completed within 7 days before arrival at [edcardaruba.aw](https://edcardaruba.aw) and not by itself a guarantee of admission); for the yacht, Customs uses **SailClear** ([sailclear.com](https://www.sailclear.com)) as a recommended pre-notice. [edcardaruba.aw ★★★★★; arubaports.com Yachting ★★★★★; Cruising sources aggregate Aruba ★★★]
- **Single port of entry for yachts: Barcadera** (about 3 NM SE of Oranjestad). While the basin is under works, the Authority may temporarily divert to the **northern basin of Oranjestad (Customs Quay / H Pier)** — in any case **always call Aruba Port Control on VHF 16/11** before entering and follow the tower's instructions; hoist a clearly visible **yellow Q flag**. [arubaports.com Yachting ★★★★★; Cruising sources aggregate Barcadera ★★★; aruba-cruisingguide.com ★★★]
- **Useful coordinates (WGS84)**: Barcadera entrance buoy **12°28.834' N 70°00.430' W**; Barcadera customs quay **12°28.855' N 70°00.014' W**; SE entrance Oranjestad **12°30.317' N 70°02.153' W**; “Hans dock” Oranjestad **12°31.285' N 70°02.709' W**. ⚠️ **Shoal <1.2 m off the NW quay**: stay in the **ship channel (≥36 ft)** until abeam the customs quay, then turn in — do not aim directly at the quay. With draught >3 m call Port Control for instructions (possible diversion to the container terminal). [arubaports.com ★★★★★; Cruising sources aggregate ★★★; aruba-cruisingguide.com “Clearing in Barcadera harbor” ★★★; blue-joline.com 2024 ★★★]
- **Alongside**: berth at the customs quay (fenders to port on entry), **only the skipper goes ashore** with documents; crew remain on board in the “yellow” area (H Pier) until clearance is completed. After formalities officers come on board for inspection (**rifles/spear guns seized**). Arrival after 22:00: designated anchorage possible with Coast Guard permission, clearance the next morning. [arubaports.com ★★★★★; varaderoaruba.com Entry Procedure ★★★; aruba-cruisingguide.com ★★★]
- **Paper forms** (printable in advance to speed up): Immigration **3 copies**, Customs **2 copies** (EN/ES/NL forms on arubaports.com). If not printed, available in the offices on the quay. Have ready: passports, approved ED cards, crew/passenger list, zarpe from the previous port (recommended). [arubaports.com Yachting — Download Forms ★★★★★; varaderoaruba.com ★★★]
- See also the full yacht procedure in [01 — Clearance](01-clearance.md).

## Staying Beyond 3 Months? (Italian/EU Citizens)

There is **no unlimited tourist stay** in Aruba as in the French Antilles: the regime is in stages with an annual cap.

| Situation | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| First 30 days | Standard admission at the border for up to 30 days (the ED card stamp is decisive) | visitaruba.com ★★★★★ |
| Extension to 90 days | Within the first 30 days you may request an extension to **90 days total**: you need **travel medical + liability insurance** valid for the extended period and **sufficient financial means** (MFA guidance: 150–200 USD/day in a hotel, 100 USD/day with private hosts; local sponsor if guaranteed). Apply at the border or immediately after entry | visitaruba.com “Extension of stay upon entry” ★★★★★; netherlandsworldwide.nl “Requirements … without a visa” (150–200 USD hotel / 100 USD private, insurance ≥15,000 USD covering hospital/emergency/repatriation) ★★★★★ |
| Extension to 180 days | Citizens of the **Kingdom of the Netherlands** may apply for up to **180 consecutive days**. Visa-exempt nationals (EU/Italy included) and those exempt via a third-country visa/residence may apply for up to **180 consecutive days if**: they **own property in Aruba** (house/apartment/timeshare **or a pleasure yacht moored in Aruba ≥14 m** at the waterline, with proof of ownership) **OR** they present a **guarantee declaration** from an Aruban resident (guarantor for all costs, to be legalised and sent via the guarantor to IASA: garantie@dga.aw). Without property, in practice an extension beyond 90 days requires a guarantor | visitaruba.com ★★★★★; netherlandsworldwide.nl ★★★★★ |
| After admission (already entered) | If you wish to stay beyond the days granted on entry, apply to **IASA — Vondellaan 25, Oranjestad, tel. +297 523-7402** (Mon–Thu 14:30–16:00 for extension requests). Documents: original extension form + copy of bio and stamped pages of passport (remaining validity ≥3 months), copy of ED card, copy of return ticket, copy of medical/liability insurance, guarantee declaration if applicable | visitaruba.com “Extension of stay after admission — IASA” ★★★★★; gobierno.aw DIMAS/IASA ★★★★★ |
| Annual cap | **Maximum 180 days per calendar year as a tourist** (even adding multiple entries). Beyond that you need a **DIMAS residence permit** (Paardenbaaistraat 11, Oranjestad, tel. +297 522-1500). Staying beyond the granted days without an extension may lead to refusal of future admissions | visitaruba.com ★★★★★; netherlandsworldwide.nl (90 days/180 days standard; 180 days/365 days for NL/US/Schengen in Aruba) ★★★★★ |
| Extension fee | **No fee** for a tourist extension application | visitaruba.com (DIMAS letter) ★★★★★ |

## The Yacht: No Unlimited Stay

**No unlimited free circulation**: a foreign yacht is admitted under **temporary import** with a cap.

| Yacht | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary import ≤180 days** | Temporary permit required if stay **>7 days**; **maximum 180 days** on the island, then the yacht must leave Aruba. No deposit if ≤180 days. Practice: declaration via **customs broker** to be started **within day 2** (broker fee **US$65–100**). On expiry, before a new permit the yacht must stay outside for **at least 15 days** | allatsea.net “NEW Aruba Customs Procedures” 2012 (still cited by local guides) ★★★; aruba-cruisingguide.com “Clearance Procedure” (yachts can stay up to 180 days) ★★★; blue-joline.com 2024 ★★★ |
| **Beyond 180 days up to 1 year** | Temporary permit with **security deposit or bank guarantee** equal to the duties due | allatsea.net — excerpt of the rules ★★★ |
| **Beyond 1 year** | **Definitive import** (customs clearance) required | allatsea.net ★★★ |
| **Ashore in entrepôt** | At **Varadero Aruba** (entrepôt area): the **180-day count is suspended** while the yacht is stored ashore. Local operational reference | aruba-cruisingguide.com “Varadero … entrepot area” ★★★; varaderoaruba.com ★★★ |
| **Environmental levy for yachts** | **DATA MISSING** — no government source found for a specific environmental levy for yachts; the *Sustainability Fee* US$20 is collected via the ED card (see below) | — |
| **Sustainability Fee US$20** | Introduced **1 July 2024**, collected via **ED Card at edcardaruba.aw** (Visa/Mastercard/Discover, line “Aruba Wastewater”). Exempt: residents, <8 years, transit <24h, serving crew, **repeat visitors within 1 year** (from 07/07/2025 automatic recognition via passport) and Aruban students abroad. For arrivals by sea the fee is due **if you complete the ED Card and clear Immigration** (yacht case); cruise passengers in transit <24h normally do not complete an ED card — check with the carrier | awss.aw FAQ ★★★★★; aruba.com Sustainability Fee ★★★★★; edcardaruba.aw ★★★★★ |
